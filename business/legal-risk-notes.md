# CharredGuard — Legal & Compliance Risk Notes (Living Doc)

**Purpose:** Track open legal/compliance questions raised against `business-plan.md`, so they don't get lost between sessions. Update the Status column as items get resolved (ideally by a CA-licensed attorney — none of this is legal advice).

**Last updated:** August 2026, after a fact-check pass against California statutes and CSLB guidance (sources cited inline).

| # | Issue | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Marketing may void the minor-work exemption** | B&P Code § 7048's minor-work exemption does **not** apply "when a person advertises or puts out a sign or card or other device that might indicate to the public that the person is a contractor." Section 6.2's worksite banners, lawn signage, and hardening-focused branding could plausibly be read that way. This isn't a technicality — it's foundational to whether the hardening add-on revenue line is legal as currently designed. | **Open — gates POAM item 2.1 (turning on hardening revenue)** |
| 2 | **Permit requirement uncertainty** | The § 7048 exemption also requires that the work "does not require a building permit." Vent retrofits and fire-hardening work in a Fire Hazard Severity Zone may require permits in some San Diego/Orange County jurisdictions — this varies by city and needs to be checked individually, not assumed. If a permit is required, the exemption doesn't apply regardless of price. | **Open — gates POAM item 0.5** |
| 3 | **"Maintain coverage" overstates 10 CCR § 2644.9** | The Safer from Wildfires regulation requires insurers to offer rate discounts for documented mitigation — it does not compel them to keep issuing or renewing a policy. Plan language (and any future client-facing copy) should stay precise about this distinction, matching the site's existing FAQ language ("neither outcome is guaranteed"). | **Noted in `business-plan.md` §1.2 — no further action needed unless client-facing copy drifts** |
| 4 | **Regulatory pin-cites may be hallucinated** | The "Underwriter Proof of Mitigation Cover Sheet" (plan §9.3) cites specific subsections like "10 CCR § 2644.9(a)(1)" and "IBHS WPH § 2.1" attached to technical claims. These were generated by an LLM (Gemini) and were not independently verified against the actual regulation/standard text. Sending a broker a document with a wrong citation is a credibility problem at minimum. | **Open — verify every citation before this template is used with a real client** |
| 5 | **Contract-splitting discipline** | The $1,000 minor-work threshold itself is confirmed accurate (raised from $500 by AB 2622, effective Jan 1, 2025 — CSLB bulletin cited below). The exemption explicitly does *not* apply when "a division of the operation is made in contracts of amounts less than $1,000 for the purpose of evasion." Billing the $500 audit separately from a hardening add-on is very likely fine (different service types — consulting vs. construction), but never split a **single hardening job's real scope** across multiple contracts/visits to keep each one under $1,000 — that specific pattern is what CSLB targets. | **Confirmed threshold + noted discipline rule — no action needed unless a job's real scope exceeds $1,000** |
| 6 | **"No employees" condition on the exemption** | Per CSLB guidance, the minor-work exemption applies "as long as the worker does not employ anyone else." If CharredGuard hires a field assistant for hardening installs (see POAM item 3.3), the exemption may no longer cover that person's work — worth re-confirming with an attorney at that hiring point, not assumed to carry over automatically. | **Open — relevant at POAM item 3.3 / 4.1** |
| 7 | **Section 9 legal templates need attorney review** | The Standard Inspection Agreement, Minor Home-Hardening Work Contract, and Underwriter Proof Cover Sheet were all Gemini-drafted. Liability waiver language, the AB 2622 statutory notice wording, and the aggregate-liability cap should all get a real attorney's sign-off before any client signs anything. | **Open — gates POAM item 0.4 / 1.1 (first paid job)** |

## Confirmed-accurate facts (no further verification needed)
- AB 2622 raised the B&P § 7048/7159 minor-work threshold from $500 to $1,000, effective January 1, 2025.
- California Insurance Code § 678 requires a 75-day written notice before non-renewal of a homeowners policy.
- 10 CCR § 2644.9 ("Safer from Wildfires") requires admitted insurers using wildfire risk scoring to offer rate discounts for documented mitigation — effective October 2022, insurer compliance required by April 2023, expanded by AB 1 effective January 1, 2026.
